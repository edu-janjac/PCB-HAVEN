What is a PCB?

What is a PCB?

A Printed Circuit Board (PCB) is a flat board that holds and connects electronic components. It is made of insulating material, usually fiberglass, with conductive pathways etched onto its surface. These pathways allow electricity to flow between the components, enabling them to work together in electronic devices.

PCBs are used in a wide range of applications, from simple gadgets to complex machinery. They provide a compact and organized way to connect electronic components, making it easier to design and manufacture electronic devices.

How it's made:

PCBs are manufactured through a multi-step process that begins with designing the circuit layout using specialized software. The design is then transferred onto a copper-clad board, where unwanted copper is chemically etched away, leaving only the desired conductive pathways. Holes are drilled for through-hole components, and the board is coated with a protective solder mask before components are soldered onto the surface.

Types of PCBs:

There are several types of PCBs, each suited for different applications. Single-layer boards are the simplest and most cost-effective, while multi-layer boards allow for more complex circuits in a smaller space. Flexible PCBs can bend and fold, making them ideal for wearable electronics and compact devices where rigid boards would not fit.
